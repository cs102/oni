1. Noun-forming suffixes

Used to create nouns from verbs, adjectives, or other nouns.

-ness → state/quality
happiness, darkness

-tion / -sion → action/result
creation, decision

-ment → result/process
development, agreement

-ity → state/condition
ability, purity

-ance / -ence → state/act
attendance, existence

-er / -or → person who does
teacher, actor

-ist → person associated with belief/activity
artist, scientist

-ism → belief/system
capitalism, realism

-ship → status/relationship
friendship, leadership

-age → collective/result
storage, marriage

-hood → state/condition
childhood, brotherhood

-cy → state/quality
accuracy, privacy

2. Verb-forming suffixes

Used to create verbs.

-ize / -ise → make/become
modernize, realise

-ify → cause to become
simplify, beautify

-ate → cause to act
activate, regulate

-en → make/become
strengthen, shorten

3. Adjective-forming suffixes

Used to create adjectives.

-ful → full of
hopeful, joyful

-less → without
careless, endless

-ous → full of/characterized by
dangerous, curious

-able / -ible → capable of
readable, flexible

-al → relating to
national, musical

-ic / -ical → relating to
historic, biological

-ive → having quality of
creative, passive

-ish → somewhat/like
childish, greenish

-y → characterized by
cloudy, funny

-ary → relating to
imaginary, dietary

4. Adverb-forming suffixes

Mostly used with adjectives.

-ly → in what manner
quickly, happily

-ward / -wards → direction
forward, sideways

-wise → in relation to
clockwise, otherwise

5. Diminutive & evaluative suffixes

Express smallness, affection, or attitude.

-let → small
booklet, streamlet

-ling → small/young
duckling, weakling

-ette → small (often French-origin)
kitchenette, cigarette

6. Comparative & superlative suffixes

Used for comparison.

-er → comparative
faster, taller

-est → superlative
fastest, tallest

7. Origin / status / specialty suffixes

Often describe nationality, ideology, or roles.

-an / -ian → person belonging to
American, historian

-ese → language/origin
Japanese, Portuguese

-ite → follower/member
Israelite, socialist

-eer → person engaged in
engineer, volunteer

8. Medical & scientific suffixes

Common in technical vocabulary.

-ology → study of
biology, psychology

-itis → inflammation
arthritis, bronchitis

-osis → condition/disease
tuberculosis, neurosis

-phobia → fear
claustrophobia

-philia → love/attraction
bibliophilia
